# Resources for mod-104-llm-benchmark-harnesses (LLM Benchmark Harnesses: lm-eval-harness, HELM, OpenAI Evals, Inspect)

Primary sources for the material in this module. Every citation is a paper, a first-party repository, or official documentation from the harness maintainers. No secondary summaries.

## Harness comparison and the four-objects taxonomy (Chapter 1)

- **EleutherAI lm-evaluation-harness.** Reference repository. [github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness). See the [`docs/`](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness/tree/main/docs) directory for the current task API and the [`new_task_guide.md`](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness/blob/main/docs/new_task_guide.md) for the YAML task-registration spec.
- **Gao, L., Tow, J., Abbasi, B., Biderman, S., Black, S., DiPofi, A., Foster, C., Golding, L., Hsu, J., Le Noac'h, A., Li, H., McDonell, K., Muennighoff, N., Ociepa, C., Phang, J., Reynolds, L., Schoelkopf, H., Skowron, A., Sutawika, L., Tang, E., Thite, A., Wang, B., Wang, K., & Zou, A. (2023).** "A framework for few-shot language model evaluation." Zenodo release, v0.4.0. [DOI:10.5281/zenodo.10256836](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.10256836). The canonical citation for lm-evaluation-harness.
- **Stanford CRFM HELM (Holistic Evaluation of Language Models).** Reference site and repository. [crfm.stanford.edu/helm/](https://crfm.stanford.edu/helm/) and [github.com/stanford-crfm/helm](https://github.com/stanford-crfm/helm). See the [HELM documentation](https://crfm-helm.readthedocs.io/) for `Scenario` / `Adapter` / `Metric` / `RunSpec` APIs.
- **Liang, P., Bommasani, R., Lee, T., et al. (2022).** "Holistic Evaluation of Language Models." *Transactions on Machine Learning Research (2023).* [arXiv:2211.09110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09110). The HELM paper.
- **OpenAI evals.** Reference repository, including the eval registry format and the `modelgraded` spec. [github.com/openai/evals](https://github.com/openai/evals) — see [`docs/`](https://github.com/openai/evals/tree/main/docs) for the eval-template reference and [`evals/registry/modelgraded/`](https://github.com/openai/evals/tree/main/evals/registry/modelgraded) for the shipped rubric library.
- **UK AI Safety Institute — Inspect.** Reference documentation and repository. [inspect.aisi.org.uk](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/) and [github.com/UKGovernmentBEIS/inspect_ai](https://github.com/UKGovernmentBEIS/inspect_ai). See the [Solvers](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/solvers.html) and [Scorers](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/scorers.html) pages for the framework's core abstractions.

## lm-evaluation-harness (Chapter 2)

- **EleutherAI lm-evaluation-harness — task API.** [`docs/new_task_guide.md`](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness/blob/main/docs/new_task_guide.md), [`docs/task_guide.md`](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness/blob/main/docs/task_guide.md), and [`docs/model_guide.md`](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness/blob/main/docs/model_guide.md). The authoritative reference for the YAML surface (`doc_to_text`, `doc_to_choice`, `output_type`, `metric_list`, filters, generation kwargs) and the `LM` adapter interface.
- **Hugging Face Open LLM Leaderboard.** The public leaderboard whose numbers are produced by lm-evaluation-harness; useful as ground-truth targets for reproduction. [huggingface.co/spaces/open-llm-leaderboard/open_llm_leaderboard](https://huggingface.co/spaces/open-llm-leaderboard/open_llm_leaderboard).
- **Fourrier, C., Habib, N., Lozovskaya, A., Hallström, K., & Tunstall, L. (2024).** "Open LLM Leaderboard v2." Hugging Face blog. [huggingface.co/blog/open-llm-leaderboard-v2](https://huggingface.co/blog/open-llm-leaderboard-v2). Documents the leaderboard-v1 → v2 rebench, including per-benchmark score deltas from harness / task changes and the specific evals (MMLU-Pro, GPQA, MuSR, BBH, IFEval, Math-Hard) that replaced the v1 suite.
- **EleutherAI Pythia paper.** **Biderman, S., Schoelkopf, H., Anthony, Q., Bradley, H., O'Brien, K., Hallahan, E., Khan, M. A., Purohit, S., Prashanth, U. S., Raff, E., Skowron, A., Sutawika, L., & Van Der Wal, O. (2023).** "Pythia: A Suite for Analyzing Large Language Models Across Training and Scaling." *ICML.* [arXiv:2304.01373](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01373). Uses lm-evaluation-harness for its full eval suite; a working example of the "publish the harness commit alongside the numbers" discipline.

## Log-likelihood vs. generation evaluators (Chapter 3)

- **Holtzman, A., West, P., Shwartz, V., Choi, Y., & Zettlemoyer, L. (2021).** "Surface Form Competition: Why the Highest Probability Answer Isn't Always Right." *EMNLP.* [arXiv:2104.08315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08315). Foundational analysis of the log-likelihood MC pathology and the PMI normalisation as a fix.
- **Zellers, R., Holtzman, A., Bisk, Y., Farhadi, A., & Choi, Y. (2019).** "HellaSwag: Can a Machine Really Finish Your Sentence?" *ACL.* [arXiv:1905.07830](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07830). The task where length-normalisation of log-likelihood MC scoring became a practical necessity; the paper documents the length-bias effect.
- **Hendrycks, D., Burns, C., Basart, S., Zou, A., Mazeika, M., Song, D., & Steinhardt, J. (2021).** "Measuring Massive Multitask Language Understanding" (MMLU). *ICLR.* [arXiv:2009.03300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.03300). The task and its canonical 5-shot prompt template; the reference for downstream discussions of the log-likelihood-vs-generation gap on MMLU.
- **Lin, S., Hilton, J., & Evans, O. (2022).** "TruthfulQA: Measuring How Models Mimic Human Falsehoods." *ACL.* [arXiv:2109.07958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.07958). Introduces the `mc1` / `mc2` metrics (single- and multi-answer log-likelihood aggregation over multiple correct candidates), a case study in bespoke normalisation.
- **Robinson, J., Rytting, C. M., & Wingate, D. (2023).** "Leveraging Large Language Models for Multiple Choice Question Answering." *ICLR.* [arXiv:2210.12353](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.12353). Direct comparison of generation-mode and probability-based MC scoring across models; documents when the two disagree and by how much.

## HELM and reproducing a public number (Chapter 4)

- **Liang, P., Bommasani, R., Lee, T., et al. (2022).** "Holistic Evaluation of Language Models." [arXiv:2211.09110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09110). The HELM paper. See especially the appendix for the `Scenario` / `Adapter` / `Metric` / `RunSpec` formal spec and the full list of scenarios and adapter flavours.
- **HELM documentation and leaderboard site.** [crfm.stanford.edu/helm/](https://crfm.stanford.edu/helm/). Includes per-scenario methodology, per-run traces (every prompt/response pair is published), and the interactive result viewer.
- **HELM code documentation.** [crfm-helm.readthedocs.io](https://crfm-helm.readthedocs.io/). The developer-facing docs for `helm-run`, `helm-summarize`, `helm-server`, and the scenario/adapter/metric registration workflow.
- **Fourrier, C., Habib, N., Lozovskaya, A., Hallström, K., & Tunstall, L. (2024).** "Open LLM Leaderboard v2." [huggingface.co/blog/open-llm-leaderboard-v2](https://huggingface.co/blog/open-llm-leaderboard-v2). The public post-mortem on why harness / task upgrades moved model rankings and forced a full rebench — the reference case for "pin the harness version aggressively."
- **Bowman, S. R., & Dahl, G. E. (2021).** "What Will it Take to Fix Benchmarking in Natural Language Understanding?" *NAACL.* [arXiv:2104.02145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02145). Motivates the reproducibility discipline the chapter argues for; the paper's checklist for a "healthy benchmark" overlaps closely with the MANIFEST from Chapter 7.
- **Reuel, A., Hardy, A., Smith, C., Lamparth, M., Hardy, M., & Kochenderfer, M. J. (2024).** "BetterBench: Assessing AI Benchmarks, Uncovering Issues, and Establishing Best Practices." *NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks.* [arXiv:2411.12990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12990). Recent survey of published benchmarks against reproducibility criteria (dataset versioning, documented decoding, published per-item traces). Useful reference for the "which of the four gap sources dominates in practice" question in exercise-02.

## Inspect (Chapter 5)

- **UK AI Safety Institute — Inspect documentation.** [inspect.aisi.org.uk](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/). The reference for `Task`, `Dataset`, `Solver`, `Scorer`, and sandboxed tool use. Key subsections: [Datasets](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/datasets.html), [Solvers](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/solvers.html), [Scorers](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/scorers.html), [Tools](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/tools.html), [Sandboxing](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/sandboxing.html), [Eval Logs](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/eval-logs.html).
- **UK AI Safety Institute — Inspect repository.** [github.com/UKGovernmentBEIS/inspect_ai](https://github.com/UKGovernmentBEIS/inspect_ai). Source of truth for the framework and its built-in solvers, scorers, and tool integrations.
- **UK AISI Inspect Evals repository.** [github.com/UKGovernmentBEIS/inspect_evals](https://github.com/UKGovernmentBEIS/inspect_evals). A large collection of real Inspect evals (agentic, safety, cyber, coding) written by UK AISI and collaborators; the best available library of "how a production Inspect eval is actually structured."
- **UK AI Safety Institute (2024).** "Pre-Deployment Evaluation of Anthropic's Upgraded Claude 3.5 Sonnet." [gov.uk report](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/pre-deployment-evaluation-of-anthropics-upgraded-claude-35-sonnet). Example of a published pre-deployment eval report built on Inspect; documents the eval methodology and result format.

## OpenAI Evals: registry and model-graded scoring (Chapter 6)

- **OpenAI evals repository.** [github.com/openai/evals](https://github.com/openai/evals). Reference for the registry YAML format, samples JSONL schema, `CompletionFn` protocol, and the shipped `Eval` and `ModelBasedClassify` classes.
- **OpenAI evals documentation.** [`docs/eval-templates.md`](https://github.com/openai/evals/blob/main/docs/eval-templates.md) covers `basic.match`, `basic.includes`, `basic.fuzzy_match`, and the `Match`-family templates. [`docs/build-eval.md`](https://github.com/openai/evals/blob/main/docs/build-eval.md) walks through registering a new eval end-to-end. [`docs/completion-fns.md`](https://github.com/openai/evals/blob/main/docs/completion-fns.md) documents `CompletionFn` extension.
- **OpenAI evals — shipped model-graded rubrics.** [`evals/registry/modelgraded/`](https://github.com/openai/evals/tree/main/evals/registry/modelgraded). Community-tested rubric templates (`fact.yaml`, `closedqa.yaml`, `battle.yaml`, `humor.yaml`, ...) — the first place to look before authoring a new one.
- **Zheng, L., Chiang, W.-L., Sheng, Y., Zhuang, S., Wu, Z., Zhuang, Y., Lin, Z., Li, Z., Li, D., Xing, E. P., Zhang, H., Gonzalez, J. E., & Stoica, I. (2023).** "Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena." *NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks.* [arXiv:2306.05685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685). The reference paper on LLM-as-judge, including position bias, verbosity bias, and self-enhancement bias. Every model-graded eval design decision in Chapter 6 traces back to a finding in this paper.
- **Panickssery, A., Bowman, S. R., & Feng, S. (2024).** "LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations." [arXiv:2404.13076](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076). Documents the self-preference bias — a grader model preferring outputs from the same model family. The reference for "do not use the same model as subject and grader" in exercise-04.
- **Wang, P., Li, L., Chen, L., Cai, Z., Zhu, D., Lin, B., Cao, Y., Kong, L., Liu, Q., Liu, T., & Sui, Z. (2023).** "Large Language Models are not Fair Evaluators." [arXiv:2305.17926](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17926). Documents position bias in pairwise LLM judging and proposes calibrated aggregation. Complementary to Zheng et al.
- **Cohen, J. (1960).** "A Coefficient of Agreement for Nominal Scales." *Educational and Psychological Measurement*, 20(1), 37–46. The original Cohen's κ, cited in the grader-vs-human validation protocol.
- **Landis, J. R., & Koch, G. G. (1977).** "The Measurement of Observer Agreement for Categorical Data." *Biometrics*, 33(1), 159–174. The κ-interpretation thresholds (`< 0.4` poor, `0.4–0.6` fair, `0.6–0.8` substantial, `> 0.8` almost perfect) used as the acceptance criterion for a graded eval.

## Prompt-format sensitivity and reproducibility (Chapter 7)

- **Sclar, M., Choi, Y., Tsvetkov, Y., & Suhr, A. (2024).** "Quantifying Language Models' Sensitivity to Spurious Features in Prompt Design or: How I learned to start worrying about prompt formatting." *ICLR.* [arXiv:2310.11324](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11324). The reference paper on prompt-template variance in LLM benchmarks; documents up-to-76-point per-model accuracy swings from semantically equivalent template changes and rank flips across models.
- **Alzahrani, N., Alyahya, H. A., Alnumay, Y., Alrashed, S., Alsubaie, S., Almushayqih, Y., Mirza, F., Alotaibi, N., Al-Twairesh, N., Alowisheq, A., Bari, M. S., & Khan, H. (2024).** "When Benchmarks are Targets: Revealing the Sensitivity of Large Language Model Leaderboards." *ACL.* [arXiv:2402.01781](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01781). Quantifies leaderboard sensitivity to small perturbations (choice-order permutations, answer-letter mapping); documents rank-flip frequencies.
- **Mizrahi, M., Kaplan, G., Malkin, D., Dror, R., Shahaf, D., & Stanovsky, G. (2024).** "State of What Art? A Call for Multi-Prompt LLM Evaluation." *Transactions of the Association for Computational Linguistics.* [arXiv:2401.00595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00595). Argues that single-prompt evaluation is misleading and proposes reporting a distribution over paraphrased prompts; the theoretical grounding for the sensitivity report in exercise-05.
- **Zhao, T., Wallace, E., Feng, S., Klein, D., & Singh, S. (2021).** "Calibrate Before Use: Improving Few-Shot Performance of Language Models." *ICML.* [arXiv:2102.09690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09690). Documents the sensitivity of few-shot performance to demonstration selection, ordering, and label distribution, and proposes contextual calibration to reduce it.
- **Lu, Y., Bartolo, M., Moore, A., Riedel, S., & Stenetorp, P. (2022).** "Fantastically Ordered Prompts and Where to Find Them: Overcoming Few-Shot Prompt Order Sensitivity." *ACL.* [arXiv:2104.08786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08786). The reference paper on few-shot ordering effects on classification benchmarks.
- **Cobbe, K., Kosaraju, V., Bavarian, M., Chen, M., Jun, H., Kaiser, L., Plappert, M., Tworek, J., Hilton, J., Nakano, R., Hesse, C., & Schulman, J. (2021).** "Training Verifiers to Solve Math Word Problems" (GSM8K). [arXiv:2110.14168](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14168). The reference for GSM8K; the task's sensitivity to `max_gen_toks` and chain-of-thought variant is why Chapter 7 uses it as a decoding-config case study.
- **Wei, J., Wang, X., Schuurmans, D., Bosma, M., Ichter, B., Xia, F., Chi, E. H., Le, Q., & Zhou, D. (2022).** "Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models." *NeurIPS.* [arXiv:2201.11903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.11903). The chain-of-thought prompting paper; frames why the CoT template variant is a first-class axis in the exercise-05 sensitivity sweep.

## Cross-cutting standards and reference implementations

- **Mitchell, M., Wu, S., Zaldivar, A., Barnes, P., Vasserman, L., Hutchinson, B., Spitzer, E., Raji, I. D., & Gebru, T. (2019).** "Model Cards for Model Reporting." *FAT\*.* [arXiv:1810.03993](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03993). The model-card standard mandates disclosure of evaluation methodology, decoding, and data splits — the same fields the Chapter 7 MANIFEST captures.
- **Gebru, T., Morgenstern, J., Vecchione, B., Vaughan, J. W., Wallach, H., Daumé III, H., & Crawford, K. (2021).** "Datasheets for Datasets." *Communications of the ACM*, 64(12), 86–92. [arXiv:1803.09010](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09010). The dataset-documentation standard cited when pinning benchmark dataset revisions.
- **NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0), 2023.** [nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework). The "Measure" function references reproducible evaluation methodology as a governance requirement.
- **Hugging Face Datasets — dataset versioning.** [huggingface.co/docs/datasets/en/loading#slicing-splits](https://huggingface.co/docs/datasets/en/loading#slicing-splits) and the `revision` argument documented in [`datasets.load_dataset`](https://huggingface.co/docs/datasets/en/package_reference/loading_methods#datasets.load_dataset). Reference for pinning `dataset_kwargs.revision` in a task YAML.
