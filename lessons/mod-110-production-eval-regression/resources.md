# Resources for mod-110-production-eval-regression (Production Evaluation and Regression Detection)

Primary sources for the material in this module. Every citation is a paper, a first-party repository, official documentation from a benchmark or platform maintainer, or a widely-cited standards document. No secondary summaries.

## Framing (Chapter 1)

- **Kohavi, R., Tang, D., & Xu, Y. (2020).** *Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments: A Practical Guide to A/B Testing.* Cambridge University Press. [experimentguide.com](https://experimentguide.com/). The reference book for controlled online experimentation; cited across Chapters 4 and 5 for pre-registration, SRM, ramp discipline, and group-sequential design. The book's chapters on measurement altitudes and organizational trust patterns are the anchor for Chapter 1's four-altitude framing.
- **Sculley, D., Holt, G., Golovin, D., Davydov, E., Phillips, T., Ebner, D., Chaudhary, V., Young, M., Crespo, J.-F., & Dennison, D. (2015).** "Hidden Technical Debt in Machine Learning Systems." *NIPS*. [papers.nips.cc/paper/2015/hash/86df7dcfd896fcaf2674f757a2463eba](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2015/hash/86df7dcfd896fcaf2674f757a2463eba-Abstract.html). The reference for why deployed-ML monitoring is a distinct discipline from offline evaluation.
- **Google SRE Book (2016).** *Site Reliability Engineering: How Google Runs Production Systems.* [sre.google/sre-book](https://sre.google/sre-book/table-of-contents/). Reference for the SLO/SLA vocabulary and the "eval alerts are process, not just statistics" disclaimer at the end of Chapter 1.

## Offline regression suites and SLO-mapped gates (Chapter 2)

- **Wilson, E. B. (1927).** "Probable Inference, the Law of Succession, and Statistical Inference." *Journal of the American Statistical Association*, 22(158), 209–212. Reference for the closed-form proportion CI used as a sanity check inside the gate-design power analysis.
- **Efron, B., & Tibshirani, R. J. (1993).** *An Introduction to the Bootstrap.* Chapman & Hall. Reference for the bootstrap variance estimate used to derive `k * σ` cushion around a baseline threshold.
- **Chow, S.-C., Shao, J., Wang, H., & Lokhnygina, Y. (2018).** *Sample Size Calculations in Clinical Research* (3rd ed.). Chapman & Hall / CRC. Reference for the power-analysis formulation the "minimum detectable delta at declared `n_min`" check in Chapter 2 §"the sample size is too small" descends from.
- **Beyer, B., Jones, C., Petoff, J., & Murphy, N. R. (2016).** *Site Reliability Engineering*, ch. 4 ("Service Level Objectives"). [sre.google/sre-book/service-level-objectives](https://sre.google/sre-book/service-level-objectives/). The SLO definition the "gate traces to an SLO" discipline is built against.
- **NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0), 2023.** [nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework). Reference for the "safety metric commitments" side of the safety-gate discussion; the "Measure" function of the framework maps to the release-gate discipline this chapter formalizes.
- **HarmBench.** [github.com/centerforaisafety/HarmBench](https://github.com/centerforaisafety/HarmBench). Mazeika et al. 2024, [arXiv:2402.04249](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04249). The `standard` split is cited in exercise-01 as the harmful-request source for the paired refusal safety gate.
- **XSTest.** [github.com/paul-rottger/xstest](https://github.com/paul-rottger/xstest). Röttger et al. 2024, [arXiv:2308.01263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01263). Over-refusal benchmark for the paired safety-gate side of exercise-01.

## Shadow and dark-launch comparisons (Chapter 3)

- **Kohavi, R., Tang, D., & Xu, Y. (2020).** *Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments*, chapters on ramping and mirror/shadow deployments. Reference for the shadow / dark-launch / mirror vocabulary distinction used at the top of Chapter 3.
- **Cameron, A. C., & Miller, D. L. (2015).** "A Practitioner's Guide to Cluster-Robust Inference." *Journal of Human Resources*, 50(2), 317–372. [jhr.uwpress.org/content/50/2/317](https://jhr.uwpress.org/content/50/2/317). Reference for the cluster-robust standard errors and cluster bootstrap that Chapter 3 §"Violation 1" uses to correct within-session correlation.
- **Efron, B., & Tibshirani, R. J. (1993).** *An Introduction to the Bootstrap*, chapter on the cluster / block bootstrap. Reference for the resample-by-cluster implementation shown in Chapter 3.
- **Chen, N., Liu, M., & Xu, Y. (2019).** "How A/B Tests Could Go Wrong: Automatic Diagnosis of Invalid Online Experiments." *WSDM*. [dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3289600.3291000](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3289600.3291000). Reference for the taxonomy of experiment-plumbing failures the "side-effect leak" and "input asymmetry" discussions inherit.
- **lmsys-chat-1m.** [huggingface.co/datasets/lmsys/lmsys-chat-1m](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lmsys/lmsys-chat-1m). Public conversational corpus recommended in exercise-02 as the base for simulated shadow triples.
- **Llama Guard 3.** [huggingface.co/meta-llama/Llama-Guard-3-8B](https://huggingface.co/meta-llama/Llama-Guard-3-8B). Meta (2024). Reference for the trained-classifier safety judge used as the refusal-detection classifier in exercise-02.

## Controlled A/B experiments with CUPED and pre-registered hypotheses (Chapter 4)

- **Deng, A., Xu, Y., Kohavi, R., & Walker, T. (2013).** "Improving the Sensitivity of Online Controlled Experiments by Utilizing Pre-Experiment Data." *WSDM*. [dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2433396.2433413](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2433396.2433413). The CUPED paper. The variance-reduction recipe, the θ estimator, and the pooled-vs-per-arm choice all derive from this reference.
- **Lin, W. (2013).** "Agnostic Notes on Regression Adjustments to Experimental Data: Reexamining Freedman's Critique." *Annals of Applied Statistics*, 7(1), 295–318. [arXiv:1208.2301](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.2301). Reference for the HC-robust regression-adjustment generalization of CUPED cited in the stretch goals of exercise-03.
- **Guo, Y., Coey, D., Konutgan, M., Li, W., Schoener, C., & Goldman, M. (2021).** "Machine Learning for Variance Reduction in Online Experiments." *NeurIPS*. [arXiv:2106.07263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07263). The MLRATE paper cited as the variance-reduction ceiling for exercise-03.
- **Kohavi, R., Tang, D., & Xu, Y. (2020).** *Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments*, §§3 (SRM), 8 (pre-registration), 22 (CUPED). Reference textbook for every discipline in Chapter 4 outside the CUPED math.
- **Rubin, D. B. (1980).** "Discussion of 'Randomization Analysis of Experimental Data: The Fisher Randomization Test' by D. Basu." *Journal of the American Statistical Association*, 75(371), 591–593. The reference for the "stable unit-treatment value assumption" (SUTVA) coined in Chapter 4's randomization-unit discussion.
- **Armitage, P., McPherson, C. K., & Rowe, B. C. (1969).** "Repeated Significance Tests on Accumulating Data." *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series A*, 132(2), 235–244. [jstor.org/stable/2343787](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2343787). The classical reference for α inflation under repeated peeking cited in Chapter 4 §"peeking without a peeking-safe test."
- **Fabijan, A., Gupchup, J., Gupta, S., Omhover, J., Qin, W., Vermeer, L., & Dmitriev, P. (2019).** "Diagnosing Sample Ratio Mismatch in Online Controlled Experiments: A Taxonomy and Rules of Thumb for Practitioners." *KDD*. [dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3292500.3330722](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3292500.3330722). Reference for the practical taxonomy of SRM causes and the p < 0.001 rule of thumb.

## Sequential testing and confidence sequences (Chapter 5)

- **Howard, S. R., Ramdas, A., McAuliffe, J., & Sekhon, J. (2021).** "Time-Uniform, Nonparametric, Nonasymptotic Confidence Sequences." *Annals of Statistics*, 49(2), 1055–1080. [arXiv:1810.08240](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08240). The reference paper for the modern confidence-sequence framework; the empirical-Bernstein construction cited in Chapter 5 comes from this line of work.
- **Howard, S. R., Ramdas, A., McAuliffe, J., & Sekhon, J. (2021).** "Uniform Chernoff and Bernstein Inequalities via Martingale Concentration." Reference for the underlying martingale-concentration machinery.
- **Waudby-Smith, I., & Ramdas, A. (2024).** "Estimating Means of Bounded Random Variables by Betting." *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series B*. [arXiv:2010.09686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.09686). The betting-based confidence sequences for bounded means; the tighter alternative to empirical Bernstein cited in Chapter 5.
- **Johari, R., Koomen, P., Pekelis, L., & Walsh, D. (2017).** "Peeking at A/B Tests: Why It Matters, and What to Do About It." *KDD*. [dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3097983.3097992](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3097983.3097992). The "always-valid p-values" reference cited in Chapter 5 alongside confidence sequences.
- **Johari, R., Pekelis, L., & Walsh, D. J. (2015; 2022 revision).** "Always Valid Inference: Continuous Monitoring of A/B Tests." *Operations Research*. [pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.2021.2135](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.2021.2135). Companion reference to the KDD paper; the operations-research treatment.
- **`confseq` Python package.** [github.com/gostevehoward/confseq](https://github.com/gostevehoward/confseq). Reference implementation by the Howard–Ramdas group for empirical-Bernstein and betting-based CS; the recommended implementation in exercise-04.
- **Lan, K. K. G., & DeMets, D. L. (1983).** "Discrete Sequential Boundaries for Clinical Trials." *Biometrika*, 70(3), 659–663. [jstor.org/stable/2336502](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2336502). The α-spending framework for group-sequential tests cited in Chapter 5.
- **O'Brien, P. C., & Fleming, T. R. (1979).** "A Multiple Testing Procedure for Clinical Trials." *Biometrics*, 35(3), 549–556. [jstor.org/stable/2530245](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2530245). The O'Brien–Fleming alpha-spending function referenced as the conservative-early / permissive-late option.
- **Pocock, S. J. (1977).** "Group Sequential Methods in the Design and Analysis of Clinical Trials." *Biometrika*, 64(2), 191–199. [jstor.org/stable/2335684](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2335684). The Pocock alternative alpha-spending function referenced alongside O'Brien–Fleming.
- **Jennison, C., & Turnbull, B. W. (2000).** *Group Sequential Methods with Applications to Clinical Trials.* Chapman & Hall / CRC. The textbook reference for group-sequential design cited in Chapter 5.
- **Benjamini, Y., & Hochberg, Y. (1995).** "Controlling the False Discovery Rate: A Practical and Powerful Approach to Multiple Testing." *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series B*, 57(1), 289–300. [jstor.org/stable/2346101](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2346101). Reference for the family-wise FDR control across a fleet of monitors in Chapter 5 §"Choosing the alpha budget."
- **Welford, B. P. (1962).** "Note on a Method for Calculating Corrected Sums of Squares and Products." *Technometrics*, 4(3), 419–420. Reference for the numerically-stable online-variance recurrence recommended for the CS implementation in exercise-04.

## Production observability and drift alerts (Chapter 6)

- **Arize Phoenix.** [github.com/Arize-ai/phoenix](https://github.com/Arize-ai/phoenix) and [docs.arize.com/phoenix](https://docs.arize.com/phoenix). Open-source LLM observability platform cited as one of the three reference platforms.
- **OpenInference specification.** [github.com/Arize-ai/openinference](https://github.com/Arize-ai/openinference). The OpenTelemetry-family convention for LLM traces cited in Chapter 6's instrumentation contract; the reference schema for span fields.
- **OpenTelemetry Semantic Conventions for GenAI.** [opentelemetry.io/docs/specs/semconv/gen-ai](https://opentelemetry.io/docs/specs/semconv/gen-ai/). The OTel-native semantic conventions the OpenInference spec aligns with.
- **Langfuse.** [github.com/langfuse/langfuse](https://github.com/langfuse/langfuse) and [langfuse.com/docs](https://langfuse.com/docs). Open-source LLM observability platform; the trace-first UI and typed Score model referenced in Chapter 6.
- **W&B Weave.** [github.com/wandb/weave](https://github.com/wandb/weave) and [wandb.github.io/weave](https://wandb.github.io/weave/). W&B's LLM observability layer; the `weave.op` decorator model referenced in Chapter 6.
- **Karnin, Z., & Liberty, E. (2015).** "Optimal Quantile Approximation in Streams." *FOCS*. [arXiv:1603.05346](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.05346). Reference for the streaming-quantile algorithms behind the dashboards' P95 / P99 displays.
- **Gretton, A., Borgwardt, K. M., Rasch, M. J., Schölkopf, B., & Smola, A. (2012).** "A Kernel Two-Sample Test." *Journal of Machine Learning Research*, 13, 723–773. [jmlr.org/papers/v13/gretton12a.html](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v13/gretton12a.html). The MMD reference for embedding-drift detection cited in Chapter 6's input-drift section.
- **Rabanser, S., Günnemann, S., & Lipton, Z. (2019).** "Failing Loudly: An Empirical Study of Methods for Detecting Dataset Shift." *NeurIPS*. [arXiv:1810.11953](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.11953). Reference survey for practical dataset-shift-detection methods (MMD, KS, classifier-based tests) the input-drift design in Chapter 6 draws from.
- **Yurochkin, M. (2018).** Population Stability Index primer at [towardsdatascience — see PSI in credit-risk classical references](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_stability_index) and Siddiqi, N. (2005) *Credit Risk Scorecards*, Wiley (chapter on population stability). PSI thresholds (0.1 moderate, 0.25 major) in Chapter 6 are the credit-risk-industry heuristic; the primary reference is Siddiqi's textbook. <!-- needs-research: a peer-reviewed primary reference for the PSI thresholds if one is expected here; the thresholds are widely-adopted heuristics rather than a formal derivation. -->
- **Sculley, D., et al. (2015).** "Hidden Technical Debt in Machine Learning Systems." Reference for the "drift is an ML-systems-design failure mode, not just a monitoring concern" framing.
- **Google SRE Book (2016).** *Site Reliability Engineering*, chapters on monitoring and alerting philosophy. [sre.google/sre-book/monitoring-distributed-systems](https://sre.google/sre-book/monitoring-distributed-systems/). The alert-fatigue / runbook discipline in Chapter 6 §"The alert-fatigue trap and how to avoid it" is the eval-side application of this book's monitoring principles.

## MLPerf-style serving benchmarks (Chapter 7)

- **Reddi, V. J., Cheng, C., Kanter, D., Mattson, P., Schmuelling, G., Wu, C.-J., Anderson, B., Breughe, M., Charlebois, M., Chou, W., Chukka, R., Coleman, C., Davis, S., Deng, P., Diamos, G., et al. - **MLCommons LoadGen documentation.** [github.com/mlcommons/inference/tree/master/loadgen](https://github.com/mlcommons/inference/tree/master/loadgen). Reference documentation for the request-generator harness cited in Chapter 7 and the stretch goal of exercise-06.

- **SGLang.** [github.com/sgl-project/sglang](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang). Zheng, L., Yin, L., Xie, Z., Sun, C., Huang, J., Yu, C. H., Cao, S., Kozyrakis, C., Stoica, I., Gonzalez, J. Alternative reference engine cited across exercise-06.

- **Hugging Face Text Generation Inference (TGI).** [github.com/huggingface/text-generation-inference](https://github.com/huggingface/text-generation-inference). Reference open-source serving engine.

- **Lin, J., Tang, J., Tang, H., Yang, S., Chen, W.-M., Wang, W.-C., Xiao, G., Dang, X., Gan, C., & Han, S. (2024).** "AWQ: Activation-aware Weight Quantization for LLM Compression and Acceleration." *MLSys*. [arXiv:2306.00978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00978). Reference for a second widely-used quantization method.
- **Dally, W. J., Turakhia, Y., & Han, S. (2020).** "Domain-Specific Hardware Accelerators." *Communications of the ACM*, 63(7), 48–57. Reference for the general hardware-benchmarking discipline the "vendor number tricks" section in Chapter 7 sits inside.
